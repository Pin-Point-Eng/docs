# What Pin is for

Pin is an operations workspace for everything happening across a property. It helps teams log, manage, track, and complete work in one place, tied directly to a live map.

Every task, request, or issue becomes a Pin. The Pin is placed at the location where the work happens, assigned to the right person or department, and tracked until it is done.

## The basic idea

A Pin is a piece of work or an issue that needs attention. A Pin can include:

- A title and description
- A location on the map
- A status
- An assignee or team
- Due dates or recurrence
- Photos, files, or notes
- Related equipment or inventory
- A chat thread
- Activity history

All communication, updates, photos, files, and coordination can stay inside the Pin, so important context is not lost in radio calls, texts, or email.

## Types of Pins

A standard Pin is a one-off task or issue created manually when work needs attention. Examples include a maintenance repair, spill cleanup, guest request, equipment issue, or security concern.

A recurring Pin is an automated checklist, inspection, patrol, or scheduled task that generates on a set cadence. It appears in the work feed like any other Pin and can be worked through and completed.

## Who uses Pin

Different people use Pin in different ways.

Workers use Pin to see assigned work, update progress, communicate, and mark tasks complete.

Managers use Pin to assign work, track open items, review team activity, and catch issues before they are missed.

Administrators use Pin to manage users, teams, locations, equipment, inventory, permissions, and settings.

Tenants or external requesters may use a request link or QR code to submit work without needing a full account.

## What Pin helps prevent

Pin is meant to reduce missed tasks, scattered messages, unclear ownership, and work that is hard to find later. Instead of chasing updates across paper forms, text messages, radio calls, or shared drives, teams can keep the work and conversation together.
